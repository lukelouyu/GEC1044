1. Thong Chai: the essentials
Singapore Thong Chai Medical Institution 新加坡同济医院 | Notes from the supplied
slide photographs and partial transcript
Main takeaway: The visit presented TCM (中医) as an individualised system of assessment
and treatment (辨证论治), delivered here through a charitable institution committed to
accessible care.
Institution and community role
Key point
What the speaker reported
History
Established in 1867, with more than 150 years of history.
Founding principle
Free medical consultation and TCM treatment for the public, regardless of race,
religion or nationality.
Mission and vision
Promote TCM, provide free consultation, treat people equally and give back to the
community; aspire to be a leading charitable TCM provider in the region.
Scale of service
More than 1,300 patients daily across its clinics, according to the briefing. This is
a visit-time statement, not an independently checked current statistic.
Clinic network
The speaker mentioned a main institution and four community clinics. Place names
in the automated transcript are unclear and are not reproduced here as confirmed
facts.
What the session covered
Assessment: the four diagnostic methods (四诊) - inspection (望诊); listening and smelling
(闻诊); inquiry (问诊); and palpation (切诊). Tongue inspection and pulse taking were the main
demonstrations.
Medicines: how Chinese medicinal materials are classified, and examples including Danggui,
Huangqi, Duzhong, Meiguihua and Chantui.
Treatments: acupuncture sensations, selected acupoints and cupping methods. These later
topics are documented mainly by photographs.
Central principle: individualised treatment
At about 34:20-35:20, the speaker explained that people with the same disease may show
different symptoms and receive different treatments. The key idea is to assess the individual
pattern rather than assume that one diagnosis always leads to one prescription.
Sources: transcribe.txt, main recording 00:00-02:48 and 34:20-35:47. The second transcript file is an identical copy.

2. Tongue diagnosis 舌诊
Observe the tongue body and its coating together.
The speaker described a normal tongue as light red with a thin white coating (薄白苔). The
tongue body (舌质) is assessed for colour, shape, size and movement; the coating (舌苔) for
colour, thickness, moisture and greasiness.
Patterns shown in the teaching slides
TCM pattern label
Appearance shown
Blood deficiency 血虚
Pale tongue; little or no coating.
Heat 热证
Red tongue; thin yellow coating.
Damp retention 湿阻
Swollen tongue; white greasy coating.
Qi stagnation 气滞
Red tongue tip.
Blood stasis 血瘀
Purple tongue; dark spots.
Damp heat 湿热
Red tongue; greasy yellow coating.
Yin deficiency 阴虚
Red tongue with cracks; little or no coating.
Qi deficiency 气虚
Pale tongue with red spots and thin white coating. The separate
common-types slide also shows teeth marks.
Yang deficiency 阳虚
Pale, swollen tongue; thin white coating and teeth marks.
How the lecturer explained the observations
Colour: paleness was linked to qi/blood deficiency, redness to heat, and purple colour to blood
stagnation within TCM theory.
Shape: swelling was associated with retained fluids or dampness; a thin tongue with
deficiency; teeth marks with spleen-qi deficiency and dampness.
Coating: yellowing was associated with heat, greasiness with dampness, and moisture or
dryness with the condition of body fluids.
What to remember for the report
These are TCM pattern descriptions presented during teaching. They are not evidence that a
tongue photograph alone can establish a medical diagnosis. The materials support describing
how practitioners interpret signs; they do not establish the accuracy of those interpretations.
Sources: Photos ending 23-47-36, 23-47-37 and 23-47-38; transcribe.txt 02:49-07:05. The first two photographs show
the same slide.

3. Pulse diagnosis 脉诊
Assess position, depth, speed, rhythm, strength and texture.
Pulse taking was demonstrated over the radial artery at the wrist. Three fingers assess cun
寸, guan 关 and chi 尺; the slide uses the radial styloid as the landmark for guan. Pressure
progresses from superficial (浮) to middle (中) to deep (沉).
Position
Left wrist: slide correspondence
Right wrist: slide correspondence
Cun 寸
Heart 心
Lung 肺
Guan 关
Liver / gallbladder 肝胆
Spleen / stomach 脾胃
Chi 尺
Kidney 肾
Kidney / life gate 肾 / 命门
These are correspondences within TCM theory. The speaker described a normal pulse as regular, neither excessively
strong nor weak, with about 4-5 beats per complete breath.
Pulse type
Description and teaching association
Superficial 浮脉
Felt with light pressure, weaker with heavy pressure; linked to exterior patterns
such as cold/flu presentations.
Deep 沉脉
Requires heavier pressure; linked to interior patterns. The depth slide distinguishes
forceful/excess from weak/deficiency.
Rapid 数脉
Slide: more than 5 beats per breath, above 90/min; linked to heat patterns.
Moderate 缓脉
Slide: relaxed and forceful, about 60-70/min; may be healthy or associated with
dampness or insufficient qi/blood.
Excess 实脉
Strong at all three positions; associated with excess patterns, but also possible in
healthy people.
Thin 细脉
Fine like a thread but still palpable; linked to qi/blood deficiency, with yin deficiency
also on the strength slide.
Wiry 弦脉
Tense like a violin string; linked to stress, anger, pain and liver/gallbladder patterns.
Slippery 滑脉
Smooth, like beads rolling on a plate; linked to phlegm/fluid or food retention and
heat; also shown for pregnancy and healthy adults.
Context matters: the slides list exercise, stress/anger, a heavy meal, age, athletic conditioning, body build and
sex-related generalisations as influences. The clearest stress-related statement is “Stress/anger: more wiry.” These
teaching ranges are not general medical screening cut-offs.
Sources: Photos ending 23-47-39 through 23-47-47; transcribe.txt 12:55-22:48. The transcript also mentions an
intermittent pulse, but its explanation is too unclear to summarise reliably.

Sensations and adverse effects discussed
The slide describes aching, numbness, distension and heaviness (酸麻胀重) as expected
needling sensations. It also lists mild pain, bleeding or bruising after removal; nausea, dizziness
or fainting; skin/allergic reactions; and infection. Fear of needles, hunger and insufficient rest
were mentioned in relation to fainting. Sterile, single-use needles were highlighted.
Cupping 拔罐 methods
Method
Description in the slide
Stationary 留罐
Cups remain in place for about 10-15 minutes.
Flash 闪罐
Cups are applied and removed quickly and repeatedly over a broad area.
Moving / gliding 走罐
Cups move along meridians for about 5-10 minutes.
The slide presents cupping as helping pain/inflammation, muscle tightness, blood flow and range of movement. A
separate colour chart assigns TCM labels to marks, including stagnation and dampness. Record these as claims shown
in teaching; the chart does not itself demonstrate that mark colour measures toxins or internal disease.

Claims to exclude or qualify
The extra file txt1.txt contains earlier commentary and suggested report prose. It is secondary
material, not a verified transcript. The supplied primary material does not establish that damp
heat is the most common pattern among Singaporean youth, that Thong Chai follows a specific
local “Qing Bu” policy, or that the speaker described a mental-health referral policy.
The slides separately map the left guan position to liver/gallbladder and link stress with wiry
pulse. They do not show that stress must produce a wiry pulse specifically at left guan.
Avoid combining these statements into a stronger claim.
