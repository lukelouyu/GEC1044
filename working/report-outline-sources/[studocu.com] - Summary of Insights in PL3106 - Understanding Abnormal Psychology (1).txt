Indicators of 
abnormality 
1. Subjective distress 
- 
Experience psychological pain e.g depression, anxiety  
2. Maladaptiveness 
- 
Interferes with our well-being and ability to enjoy work and 
rlships 
- 
e.g. breaking chairs when angry is not a useful behaviour  
- 
e.g. people with depression may withdraw from family; people 
with anorexia restricts food intake till emacication  
- 
Maladaptive for and toward society:  
- 
e.g. contract killers who kill for payment  
3. Statistical deviancy  
- 
Statistically rare and undesirable  
- 
E.g. severely diminished intellectual functioning VS statistically 
rare and highly desirable genius 
4. Violation of standards of society 
- 
Fail to follow conventional social and moral rules of their 
cultural grp; not acceptable in society  
- 
e.g. removing clothes in class 
- 
Depends on magnitude of violation and how commonly the 
rule is violated by others 
● 
E.g. parking illegally VS mother drowning her child 
5. Social discomfort 
- 
Cause a sense of discomfort or unease to people around 
6. Irrationality and unpredictability  
- 
No logical thinking, unpredictable and makes no sense 
- 
E.g. Explosive at the smallest things, suddenly shouting aloud 
in class  
- 
Depends on whether person can control behaviour  
7. Dangeousness  
- 
Cause risk to themselves or people around them 
● 
E.g. suicide  
 
Person may have one or more of these criteria -> LOOK AT BIGGER 
PICTURE  
Main areas of 
impairment 
1. Academic functioning  
2. Social functioning 
3. Family functioning  
4. Occupational functioning (work) 
 
In addition,

Behaviour therapy 
Modify and shape behaviour by establishing relationship behaviour and 
outcome  
- 
E.g child formed relationship between whining and getting what he 
wants -> need to break that relationship  
- 
Usually use with CHILDREN + people with intellectual disability  
 
Techniques:  
- 
Exposure therapy (further techniques within: systematic 
desensitzation, flooding) 
● 
Use for fears and anxieties -> expose indivs to what they are 
most afraid of  
● 
If you mention exposure therapy, SPECIFY TO WHAT  
 
- 
Token economy  
● 
You have to do sth then you get tokens -> then exchange 
tokens for sth you value (e.g money) 
 
- 
Social skills training  
● 
Teaching person social skills  
● 
Sb with social anxiety may not need social skills training -> 
cos their challenge in social situations is about how the person 
is viewing them and how the other person may judge them -> 
may not be because they lack social skills  
 
- 
Systematic reinforcement  
● 
When i see the behaviour of interest (e.g being polite), i 
reinforce the behaviour (e.g praise behaviour) 
 
- 
Problem-solving  
● 
Esp for those with intellectual disability -> e.g. if you arrive at 
the MRT and have no MRT card, what do you do? 
 
- 
Role-playing  
● 
We have a scenario and person practices the skill 
● 
Can use for social anxiety  
 
- 
Modelling  
● 
Model the required skill 
 
Many of the techniques are paired together!!

Cognitive therapy 
Identifying and challenging unhelpful thinking patterns to improve emotional 
responses and behaviours  
 
Techniques:  
- 
Thought recording 
● 
Get client to track their thoughts -> write down what was the 
situation, what were you thinking, what were you feeling  
 
- 
Cognitive restructuring  
● 
Taking closer look at the thought -> is it magnifying or 
catastrophizing -> challenge them to move this thought to be 
more in perspective or realistic -> e.g doesn’t mean that 
everyone has to love what I’m saying and doesn’t mean that i 
am the most boring person cos I do have some friends  
● 
Bringing them from the furthest end to the middle, not the 
other end (cos it’s still unrealistic)  
● 
Myth of CBT: causes you to be overly optimistic but NOT 
TRUE -> just need to bring client to a place more reflective of 
reality

Gestalt therapy  
- 
Emphasizes personal responsbility and focuses on an individual’s 
present experiences and challenges rather than delving deeply into 
past events 
- 
Focusing on here and now, rather than past experiences  
- 
Helps client uncover why they do what they do  
Psychodynamic 
therapies  
Focus on interplay between unconscious processes and conscious thoughts, 
emotions and behaviours.  
- 
Consider unresolved conflicts, how childhood experiences influence 
how you are now and unconscious motivations 
- 
How past events relate to current events  
 4 basic techniques: 
1. Free association 
- 
Indiv must say wtv comes into her/his mind, regardless of how 
personal, painful or seemingly irrelevant it may be  
- 
Purpose: explore thoroughly contents of preconscious  
2. Analysis of dreams 
- 
When person is asleep, repressive defenses are said to be 
lowered and forbidden desires and feelings may find an outlet 
in dreams (“royal road to unconscious”) 
- 
Dream has 2 kinds of content: 
1. Manifest content: dream as it appears to dreamer  
2. Latent content: actual motives that are seeking 
expression but are so painful/unacceptable that 
they’re disguised  
3. Analysis of resistance  
- 
To understand why certain topics are hard to bring up  
- 
During process of free association or of associating to dreams, 
an indiv may show resistance: an unwilligness/inability to talk 
abt certain thoughts, motives or experiences 
- 
Resistance may also be evidenced by client’s viging a too-glib 
interpretation of some association, coming late to an appt or 
even forgetting the appt  
4. Analysis of transference  
- 
Transference: people often unconsciously apply to their 
therapist attitudes and feelings they haven had in past rlships

Treatments  
 
ERP: Specailised form of CBT specifically designed to treat OCD  
Key components: 
- 
Exposure: Exposing individuals to situations or thoughts that trigger 
their obsessions  
- 
Response prevention: Prevent individual from engaging the 
compulsive response. By resisting the urge to perform rituals or 
compulsions, individuals learn over time that their anxiety will decrease 
over time w/o the need for these compulsions 
- 
E.g sb with a fear of contamination and is asked to touch a door knob 
in a library and then refrains from washing their hands immediately 
 
People with OCD usually recognise that they dont need to engage in such 
behaviours but they can’t help themself from checking -> they know it’s illogical 
-> it’s uncomfortable for them to not check  
BDD 
Preoccupation with a perceived defect or flaw in physical appearance that is 
not observable or appears slight to others  
 
- 
Repetitive behaviours  
- 
Distress 
- 
“Hidden” disorder 
- 
Onset during adolescence, chronic course  
- 
2-5%, high risk & disability

Types of disorders 
Separation anxiety 
disorder 
Excessive anxiety about separation from major attachment figures such as 
mother and from familiar home surroundings  
- 
Major attachment figures are usually their main caregiver -> they get 
anxious when person leaves and worry person won’t come back  
- 
Lacks self-confidence, apprehensive in new situations, immature for 
age 
- 
Slightly more common in girls than boys, with time the disorder may go 
away on its own  
- 
More likely to also experience other anxiety-based disorders such as 
phobia and OCD 
 
Causal factors in anxiety disorders: 
- 
Anxious children often show early sensitivity -> temperament -> 
sensitive to feedback  
- 
Experience of trauma thru hospitalization or violence  
- 
Parents can foster anxiety in children in they way they behave towards 
situation (e.g overanxious or overprotective parents) -> children model 
after them -> OR if parents are rejecting, indifferent 
- 
Cultural expectations and dffs lead to diff patterns of anxiety in children 
 
Treatment: 
- 
CBT has been effective at reducing anxiety symptoms in young 
children 
- 
Exposure-based treatment is effective in treatment of adult, adolescent 
and child anxiety 
Depression  
Childhood depression is characterized by symptoms of sadness, withdrawal, 
crying, poor sleep and appetite and in some cases, thoughts of suicide or 
suicide attempts  
- 
Irritabiltiy is often a major symptom;  can be substitiuted for depressed 
mood 
 
Treatment:  
- 
Antidepressants are used, but associated with increased risk of 
suicidal thoughts and behaviours  
- 
CBT 
Conduct problems

Recurrent pattern of negativistic, defiant, disobedient and hostile behaviour 
toward authority figures that persists for >6months 
- 
Subtypes: 
1. Angry/irritable mood 
2. Argumentative/defiant behave
