1. Introduction
The field trip to Thong Chai Medical institution was an immersive and unique opportunity, where
I could gain a more tangible sense of TCM beyond our usual online lectures and
classroom-based tutorials. This visit allowed me to learn more about the medical institution and
gain hands-on experience for techniques used in TCM. In this field trip report, I will be
explaining my experience and personal reflections during this field trip in detail..
2. Introducing Thong Chai Medical Institution
During this field trip, I learnt more about Thong Chai Medical Institution through Dr. Grace, who
is one of the TCM practitioners there. She explained its history, of how it was originally
established as a free clinic with the ideals to treat all equally and to help without discrimination1.
Over the years, the medical institution also played an integral role in educating more people
about TCM, through scholarships, seminars, herb displays and even a TCM library2 (Figure 1).
Today, the medical institution provides various TCM services, such as free medical consultations
and herbal medication.
Figure 1: The current TCM library at Thong Chai Building3.
Seeing the humble and nondescript exterior of the current Thong Chai Building (Figure 2), I was
surprised yet captivated as I learnt about the long and rich history that this medical institution
had. It also made me more grateful that students, such as I, have the opportunity to visit this
medical institution and learn more about TCM here.

Tongue diagnosis was easier to discern and I had fun utilising the knowledge that I gained

characteristics. However, one challenge that I faced while observing the tongues of my friends

3.2 Pulse Taking
Next, Dr. Grace went in depth about pulse taking and diagnosis. She shared about the different
characteristics of a pulse and the various indicators in each characteristic, which includes:
1.
Depth
a.
FuMai - pulse felt strongly from the surface, indicating external invasion
b.
ChenMai - pulse only felt after pressing deeply, indicating a weak constitution
2.
Speed
a.
ShuoMai - rapid pulse, indicating heat syndromes
b.
HuanMai - moderate pulse, indicating healthy pulse or syndromes of damp,
insufficient Qi and Blood
c.
JieMai or DaiMai - pulse stopping at intermittent intervals, indicating heart
problems, poor health, or trauma conditions
3.
Strength
a.
ShiMai - Excess or Strong pulse, indicating excess syndrome or healthy
b.
XiMai - fine or thin pulse, indicating Qi and Blood deficiency or Yin Deficiency
4.
Special characteristics
a.
XianMai - stringy and long pulse, indicating stress, anger, liver and gallbladder
disease or abnormal circulation of Qi
b.
HuaMai - Slippery pulse, feels like a rolling bead, indicating fluid retention,
excess heat, pregnant women or healthy
She also showed us the different pulse positions that corresponded to different meridian points
(Figure 4) and the different factors that pulse can be influenced by.

For this diagnosis method, it was easy for me to find my pulse, but very difficult to identify

Wayne’s point (from Lecture 7 on TCM diagnostics) that only by feeling a thousand pulses can
we truly learn to distinguish between a normal and abnormal pulse.

5. Acupuncture
After going through the various herbs displayed to us, Dr. Grace taught us about acupuncture,
its uses and things to look out for during acupuncture. Acupuncture can be used for pain, stroke
recovery, insomnia, migraines, anxiety, depression, sinus congestion and more. Meanwhile,
things to look out for include bleeding or bruising, skin rashes, nausea or infection. Dr. Grace
then showed us a few acupoints:
1.
百会- on the head, for headache, dizziness, insomnia, tinnitus, dementia, stroke
2.
曲池- outer elbow, for pain relief in the elbow and arm, heatiness, toothache, sore throat
3.
合谷- skin between index finger and thumb, for heatiness, headache, toothache, eye
infections, digestive issues
4.
足三里- anterior aspect of the lower leg, for digestive issues, boost immunity
5.
太冲- dorsum of the foot, for liver Qi stagnation (stress, irritability, menstrual
irregularities)
I did not take the opportunity to try acupuncture as I was quite apprehensive of needles.
However, I could observe my friend getting acupuncture at her 百会point, and she shared that it
was actually quite painless. Prof. Charles Wong also shared that acupuncture needles were
much thinner than other needles used for injections, so acupuncture would hurt much less.
Nonetheless, I was also glad that we can massage the acupoints as an alternative.
6. Cupping
Lastly, Dr. Grace taught us what cupping is, its uses, different methods of cupping and what to
look out for during a cupping session. Cupping acts as a deep tissue massage that helps with
pain, inflammation, blood flow, relaxation and well-being. There were 3 different methods of
cupping: stationary cupping (cups are left on the body), flash cupping (cups are applied and
removed quickly) and gliding cupping (cups are moved along the meridians). Dr. Grace also
showed us a diagram explaining what different cupping colours indicated (Figure 5).

Next, Dr. Grace invited one of the students to participate in a cupping demonstration. This was
the first time that I have seen cupping done in real life and I found it fascinating to watch how
skillfully she applied each cup. The student that volunteered for the demonstration was quite
healthy, as his skin after the cupping had a colouring that fit a balanced body type. Meanwhile,
Prof. Charles Wong shared about another student’s demonstration, where one spot of the
student’s skin was grey after the cupping, as it was placed over the area that he was injured at.
This showed me the effectiveness of TCM techniques in revealing imbalances or injuries within
the body.
7. Conclusion
This field trip to Thong Chai Medical Institution was an eye-opening journey into the world of
Traditional Chinese Medicine. Trying different diagnosis methods, observing different herbs and
seeing acupuncture and cupping up close, made TCM feel much more tangible. I also enjoyed
discovering the history of Thong Chai Medical Institution and its commitment to accessible care,
which gave me a new appreciation for TCM. This experience has left me curious and excited to
learn more about how TCM can complement modern medicine and how to incorporate TCM into
my daily life.
