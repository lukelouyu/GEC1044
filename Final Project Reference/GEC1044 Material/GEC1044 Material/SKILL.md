## Tong Keow project context

The case is **Tong Keow Healthcare, 同侨保健药行**, at **Block 809 Hougang Central, #01-170, Singapore 530809**, as identified in the supplied project files. The fieldwork account and photographs concern a **13 September 2026** visit. The working research question examines how inherited knowledge, neighbourhood relationships, retail convenience and local adaptation sustain TCM in Singapore.

Useful analytical lenses include:

- **Yangsheng and food therapy:** food-like herbs and soups make cultivation of health part of household routines.
- **Individualization and standardization:** pattern differentiation considers a particular person; fixed packaged products offer convenience and repeatability.
- **Localization:** imported ingredients, local manufacture/packing, tropical-climate explanations and cross-community shopping are different dimensions of adaptation.
- **Retail–clinical boundaries:** selling, weighing or recommending products does not demonstrate clinical diagnosis or a registered physician at this outlet.
- **Trust and commerce:** staff explanation, product presentation and preparation effort may affect accessibility; support these interpretations with specific evidence.

The preparation package starts with `[data not obtained]` and hypothetical/planned activities. Interviews, photographs and later documents exist elsewhere in the folder. Do not copy the preparation status as the final evidence status, and do not turn its proposed questions or hypotheses into findings.

## Evidence distinctions that matter

1. **Rankings:** the transcripts do not establish audited top-eight sales rankings. Attribute lists to staff recollection or reported examples unless records supply the metric, period and ordering. The preparation code A/B/C/D means documentary evidence, record-informed estimate, experienced recollection and observational proxy respectively. It is a project logging convention, not a validation score already assigned to every response.
2. **Soups:** the guide says the interview named six common ingredients, not five verified recipes or ranked therapies: dang shen, huang qi, yu zhu, Chinese yam, red dates and goji berries. Do not invent fixed combinations, doses or sales ranks.
3. **Local production:** separate grown, manufactured, formulated, packed and merely sold in Singapore. Cactus and roselle were mentioned as locally linked examples; exact supply chains were not independently established. An imported herb in a local pack is not locally grown.
4. **Clinical status:** lack of confirmation is not proof of absence. Verify whether an in-house registered physician operates at this branch before declaring the clinical tables applicable or not applicable.
5. **Brands and ingredients:** interview categories such as liver-care or mood-support products do not identify one formula. Do not assign exact ingredients or brands without a readable label or another specific source. The two medicated oils' exact labels were not recorded in the guide.
6. **Popularity and effectiveness:** staff-reported popularity, traditional indications, label claims and demonstrated biomedical effects are different. Describe qi support, yin nourishment, heat-clearing and light nourishment as TCM concepts or attributed interpretations.
7. **Demographics:** the soup guide uses 2020 Census figures for Hougang Planning Area and Hougang Central subzone. These are distinct geographic denominators and historical population context, not a measured customer profile.
8. **Customer/market claims:** one staff interview and a short visit cannot establish population-wide preferences, age distributions or causal effects of delivery platforms. Confirm whether an activity was observed, staff-reported, researched externally, or merely proposed.

The herb guide covers ginseng, American ginseng, dang shen, huang qi, yu zhu, Chinese yam, dates, goji, monk fruit, lingzhi and ban zhi lian. Use that file for the detailed attributed functions. Lower demand for specialist herbs or stronger tonics is an interpretation requiring staff evidence, not a general pharmacological ranking. “Qing bu” means light nourishment in the project discussion; it is not a modern effectiveness rating.

## Course concepts and source integrity

Week 1 readings concern pattern thinking and medicine–society relations; Week 2 Daoism and canon formation; Week 3 Song–Qing medical change; Week 4 creative Western reception; Week 5 Singapore institutions and localization. Match claims to the actual reading/lecture, not just a general label of “holism.” Preserve differences between Kaptchuk's teaching contrast and Unschuld's critique of simplified East/West binaries.

TCM functional organs, qi, yin–yang and traditional pathogenic categories are not automatic equivalents of anatomy, physical energy or infectious agents. Historical authority, practitioner registration and product listing do not establish clinical effectiveness. Use the external original source for professional, regulatory or empirical claims and verify current details when needed.

For fieldwork writing, connect **specific evidence → interpretation → course concept → limitation**. Cite images, interview passages, readings and external research according to their actual provenance. Do not invent quotations, sales numbers, demographics, brands, physician confirmation or therapeutic outcomes. Avoid exposing customer health details, faces or private records when reusing evidence.
