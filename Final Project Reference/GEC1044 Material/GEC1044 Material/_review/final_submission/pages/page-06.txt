Product form and intended use also shape regulation. Finished Chinese Proprietary Medicines
(CPMs) require HSA product listing and the relevant dealer licence (HSA, 2026a, 2026b). Raw
medicinal materials and culinary foods fall under separate categories. Permitted materials may be
used in soups or drinks for non-medicinal purposes (Singapore Food Agency, 2025). Tong Keow
displays these products together on its shelves, but each product type retains its own regulatory
status.
5.2 Packaging and the language of trust
Packaging makes product information visible. CPM labels must provide English information on
ingredients, batch numbers, expiry dates and supply-chain details (HSA, 2025a, section 5). For
CPMs, these requirements support identification and traceability. Buyers can identify the product and
its source. HSA listing addresses safety and quality, but does not demonstrate clinical effectiveness
(HSA, 2026a).
Staff emphasised fresh colour and sulphur-free materials. Field photographs show a sulphur-free
claim on Dang Shen and a separate American ginseng label. The first presents a processing claim,
while the second identifies the material. Together, they show how labels build shopper confidence by
communicating processing information and product identity.
5.3 Different shoppers in a shared space
Staff said most customers were Chinese, while some Malay customers bought dessert ingredients
and some Indian customers bought nuts. Everyday foods may give different communities familiar
reasons to enter the shop. These purchases do not require a shared understanding of TCM. The
mixed range therefore suggests several household uses within one retail space.
Course material on senna shows that the same plant can have different meanings in Chinese and
Malay medicine. This helps explain how similar materials can serve different cultural routines. But
purchases alone cannot establish cultural exchange or reveal shoppers’ beliefs. Customer interviews
would show whether buyers regard these products as food, medicine or both.
