2.3 Hougang and the customers described by staff
Staff described mainly Chinese, middle-aged and older customers, including people in their forties
and fifties. Hougang Central had about 4,620 residents in June 2025. Around 1,070 were aged 65 or
above (23%) (Singapore Department of Statistics [DOS], 2025). These figures give neighbourhood
context, but do not identify actual shoppers.
Hougang also has a rich Teochew history. The National Heritage Board describes the community
around Tou Mu Kung and its fishing, farming and rubber-growing activities (National Heritage Board
[NHB], n.d.). The residential setting and heritage photograph place the shop within this local history.
3 Everyday care and food therapy
3.1 Herbs in household meals
Staff named Dang Shen (党参), Huang Qi (黄芪), Yu Zhu (玉竹), Huai Shan (淮山), red dates (红枣)
and goji (枸杞) as common soup ingredients. They also described ginseng (人参), American ginseng
(西洋参) and monk fruit (罗汉果) as common purchases. Table A1 records nine profiles.
Soup gives herbs a familiar household purpose. The lecture describes Chinese medicine as a
cultural framework shaping food and health habits. Nourishing life (养生) and medicine and food
sharing a source (药食同源) help explain this connection. Lim et al. (2005) identify health
maintenance as an important reason for dietary herb use. Cooking offers one route for this practice:
households use traditional ingredients in meals, not only when seeking treatment. A familiar meal
gives nourishment a practical place in daily life, so using herbs need not begin with a specific illness.
