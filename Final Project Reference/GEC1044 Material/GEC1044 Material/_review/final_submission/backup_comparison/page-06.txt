3.2 Soup packs and light nourishment
Our field survey grouped the soup products under five labels: Qing Bu Liang (清补凉), Dang
Shen–Huang Qi soup (党参北芪补气汤), Yu Zhu–Huai Shan soup (玉竹淮山润燥汤), goji–red date
soup (枸杞红枣养生汤), and Five Finger Fig soup (五指毛桃健脾汤). As recorded in Table A2, staff
linked these preparations to specific reported purposes, such as aiding digestion (助消化),
moistening (润燥), and light nourishment (qing bu / 清补). In TCM, light nourishment can be
presented as an everyday dietary option alongside stronger tonification (da bu / 大补). Pre-packaged
ingredient packs make these ideas easier to use because households receive preselected, portioned
ingredients and still complete the cooking at home.
Several of these soups, especially Five Finger Fig soup, are framed around Spleen/digestive
support. The TCM Spleen refers to a broader functional system rather than the anatomical organ
(WHO, 2022). However, the five labels also include moistening and general nourishment purposes,
so they should not all be reduced to a single organ system.
