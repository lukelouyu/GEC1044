4.3 Preparation effort and staff-described lower-demand products
Lower-demand examples included Ban Zhi Lian (半枝莲), specialist herbs requiring combinations
and strong tonic formulas (大补方) (Table A4). Staff associated their lower appeal with unfamiliarity
and preparation effort, and considered stronger tonics less suitable for Singapore’s hot, humid
climate than lighter qing bu (清补) options. Slower-selling items were stocked in smaller quantities.
This comparison helps explain the appeal of prepared packs: shoppers receive selected, portioned
ingredients instead of assembling combinations themselves. It also suggests that staff guidance and
preparation services matter alongside the ingredients’ traditional-use descriptions.
5 Local adaptation through products, packaging and cultural contact
5.1 Imported materials and locally made products
Staff reported mainly Chinese imports for variety and cost, with limited local cultivation. Table A5
separates three meanings of “local”: grown, packaged or manufactured in Singapore. Cactus and
roselle illustrate possible local cultivation, but shop-stock origins remain unconfirmed. Mind Soothe
Capsules have a documented Singapore manufacturing link. Five Flower Tea and monk-fruit tea are
external examples of local packaging and beverage production, not Tong Keow products. These
distinctions illustrate adaptation through Singapore’s retail and production systems, even with
imported ingredients (Yang, 2022).
HSA lists Nature’s Green Mind Soothe Capsules as manufactured by Tong Jum Chew in Singapore,
without establishing ingredient origins (HSA, 2025b). Product categories also have different
requirements. Finished Chinese Proprietary Medicines (CPM) require listing and dealer licences;
manufacturing and assembly facilities must meet Good Manufacturing Practice requirements (HSA,
2026b, 2026c). Raw or simply processed medicinal herbs are Chinese medicinal materials, while
permitted ingredients in culinary soups or drinks for non-medicinal use are food products (Singapore
Food Agency, 2025). Local manufacture therefore does not determine a product’s regulatory
category.
5.2 Packaging and the language of trust
Packaging makes product information visible to shoppers. CPM labels require English; Chinese may
be added, while other languages must match approved English. Product names, ingredients, batch
numbers, expiry dates and manufacturer, importer or assembler details help identify products and
trace their supply chains (HSA, 2025a, section 5). These details can support trust, but HSA listing
concerns safety and quality, rather than proving effectiveness or individual suitability (HSA, 2026b).
Consumer guidance also highlights possible interactions with other medicines (HSA, 2026d).
