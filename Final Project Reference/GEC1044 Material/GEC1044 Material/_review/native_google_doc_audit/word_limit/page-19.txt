Diarrhoea
Huoxiang Zhengqi type
remedies
Mei Hua Huoxiang Zhengqi contains
patchouli, perilla leaf, angelica dahurica
root, atractylodes, tangerine peel, pinellia,
magnolia bark, poria, platycodon, licorice,
areca peel, jujube and ginger. Science Arts
describes dampness-related presentations
involving vomiting, diarrhoea, abdominal
fullness and body heaviness. The reference
context distinguishes a symptom cluster
from diarrhoea considered alone.
Fatigue or Qi deficiency
language
Ginseng or Dang Shen
Ginseng and Dang Shen are separate
Qi-tonifying materials rather than one fixed
formula. PolyU describes ginseng as
supporting original Qi and Spleen–Lung
functions, and Dang Shen as supporting
Spleen and Lung Qi. Reference symptoms
include poor appetite, shortness of breath
and, for Dang Shen, loose stools; these
provide context beyond fatigue alone.
Perceived lower immunity
Lingzhi or supplements
Reference Lingzhi is the dried fruiting body
of Ganoderma lucidum or G. sinense.
PolyU describes traditional Qi-tonifying and
calming uses, with reference symptoms
including insomnia, palpitations and
shortness of breath. “Lower immunity” is a
broad retail description and does not
specify a single formula, TCM pattern or
clinical diagnosis.
Late nights, stress and
long work hours
Liver care or mood
support products
Mind Soothe is a 16-ingredient example;
key ingredients listed by Tong Jum Chew
include bupleurum, acorus, lily bulb, sour
jujube seed, curcuma, polygala, licorice and
jujube. The manufacturer describes
Liver-constraint and restlessness-related
uses. Its reference symptom context
includes sleep and mood concerns; late
nights or long work hours alone do not
establish that pattern.
