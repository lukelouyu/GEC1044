5.2 Packaging and the language of trust
CPM labels require English, ingredient, batch, expiry and supply-chain information; Chinese may be
added, while other languages must match approved English (HSA, 2025a). Traceability and listing
concern quality, not individual suitability (HSA, 2026b, 2026d).
Quality labels communicate a further kind of assurance. The staff we interviewed stressed fresh
colour and sulphur-free materials. Figure 5.2a shows HSA signage, while Figure 5.2b shows a
sulphur-free Dang Shen label and a separate American ginseng label. The Dang Shen label
presents a processing claim, while the American ginseng label identifies the product; together, these
labels show how specific wording may reassure shoppers. The photograph records these claims
without verifying them. In its separate guidance on health supplements, HSA also prohibits
suggesting that a product is safe simply because it is natural (HSA, 2026a). The distinction between
a label’s message and independently established product properties is therefore central to
interpreting the language of trust.

5.3 Different shoppers in a shared space
Staff described some Malay customers buying dessert ingredients and Indian customers buying
nuts. Food may enable shared access, while meanings differ across medical traditions (GEC1044
Lecture 5, n.d.).
