1 Introduction
Tong Keow’s Hougang outlet sells herbs and soup packs alongside everyday foods. We argue that
this retail setting keeps Traditional Chinese Medicine (TCM) visible and usable in household
routines.
The report examines food therapy, convenience and local adaptation, while distinguishing retail
descriptions from clinical treatment.
2 Fieldwork and the neighbourhood medicine hall
2.1 How we conducted the fieldwork
We visited Tong Keow on 13 September 2026, photographed displays and interviewed one staff
member. Transcripts, photographs and public sources inform this report; demand descriptions reflect
staff experience.
Observations, staff accounts and our interpretations are distinguished throughout.
2.2 Tong Keow and its mixed retail model
According to its company profile, Tong Keow Medical Hall is a TCM shop and clinic with roots in Toa
Payoh. The profile states that it was founded in 1968 and is now run by the second generation of
family owners. Its promotional materials describe raw herbs, such as American ginseng and monk
fruit, alongside cooling teas, pills and medicinal plasters (Tong Keow Medical Hall, 2022; “Instagram
Reel About Tong Keow Medical Hall,” n.d.).
We observed retail activity, without verifying advertised services or recording consultations.
Branded soup packs beside grains and foods connect herbal shopping with ordinary grocery
purchases.
