3 Everyday care and food therapy
3.1 Herbs in household meals
Staff identified nine popular herbs (Table A1), including Dang Shen, Huang Qi and ingredients
commonly cooked in soups.
Soup gives herbs a familiar household purpose. Lecture 5 describes Chinese medicine as a cultural
framework that shapes everyday food and health habits (GEC1044 Lecture 5, n.d.). This helps us
understand the display through the ideas of nourishing life (养生) and medicine and food sharing a
source (药食同源). Lim et al. (2005) identify health maintenance as an important reason for the use
of herbs in daily food intake. Our fieldwork shows one way this happens: herbs enter the household
through cooking.
3.2 Soup packs and light nourishment
Our field survey grouped the soup products under five labels: Qing Bu Liang (清补凉), Dang
Shen–Huang Qi soup (党参北芪补气汤), Yu Zhu–Huai Shan soup (玉竹淮山润燥汤), goji–red date
soup (枸杞红枣养生汤), and Five Finger Fig soup (五指毛桃健脾汤). As recorded in Table A2, staff
linked these preparations to specific reported purposes, such as aiding digestion (助消化),
moistening (润燥), and light nourishment (qing bu / 清补). In TCM, light nourishment can be
presented as an everyday dietary option alongside stronger tonification (da bu / 大补). Pre-packaged
ingredient packs make these ideas easier to use because households receive preselected, portioned
ingredients and still complete the cooking at home.
Purposes include digestion, moistening and nourishment. The TCM Spleen denotes a functional
system, rather than the anatomical organ (WHO, 2022).
