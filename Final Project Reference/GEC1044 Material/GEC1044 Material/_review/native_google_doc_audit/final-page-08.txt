4 Convenience and changing ways of using products
4.1 Easier preparation for younger buyers
Staff described younger customers as buying fewer raw herbs and preferring teas, supplements and
simpler preparations. Table A3 keeps top eight selling products, including ginseng tonics, herbal
teas, soup packs, mood-support products and medicated oils. This account highlights product form
because staff link easier preparation to younger customers’ routines.
Soup packs show how products meet this preference for convenience. The ingredients are already
well-selected and well-portioned, so households can skip the initial preparation of raw herbs and
start cooking. Staff explain the intended use of specific ingredients, and buyers finish preparing the
soup at home. Finished capsules need even less effort. The photographed multi-buy promotion also
presents soup packs through a familiar supermarket offer. We see convenience as a change in how
the work is shared between the seller and the household. The shop takes on more of the selection
and preparation, making traditional ingredients easier to choose and use in daily routines.
