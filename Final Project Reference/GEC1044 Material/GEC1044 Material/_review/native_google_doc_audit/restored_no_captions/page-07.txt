Finished Chinese Proprietary Medicines (CPM), including capsules and tablets, require the relevant
dealer licences and product listing for sale and supply in Singapore (HSA, 2026b). Local
manufacturing and assembly facilities must also meet HSA’s Good Manufacturing Practice
requirements (HSA, 2026c). HSA’s dataset lists Mind Soothe Capsules (舒肝解郁胶囊), under the
Nature’s Green (绿叶) brand, with Tong Jum Chew Pte Ltd as manufacturer and Singapore as the
manufacturing country (HSA, 2025b). This establishes a manufacturing link, not the origin of every
ingredient. Under Singapore’s regulatory classification, raw or simply processed herbs intended for
traditional medicinal use fall within Chinese medicinal materials; when permitted materials are
incorporated into culinary soup packs or herbal drinks for non-medicinal use, the finished items are
classified as food products. Their classification therefore depends on their form and intended use
(Singapore Food Agency, 2025).
5.2 Packaging and the language of trust
Packaging identifies a product’s contents and source. HSA’s Chinese Proprietary Medicines
guidelines require full English labelling. Chinese may be added; information in other languages must
match the approved English labelling. Labels must provide product and ingredient details, batch and
expiry information, and the relevant manufacturer, importer or assembler details (HSA, 2025a,
section 5). These requirements make the product and its supply chain more traceable. Product
listing addresses safety and quality requirements (HSA, 2026b), while HSA’s consumer guidance
also notes possible interactions with other medicines (HSA, 2026d). For this report, traceable
product information supports an analysis of how trust is communicated; it does not establish
suitability or treatment effectiveness for an individual shopper.
Quality labels communicate a further kind of assurance. The staff we interviewed stressed fresh
colour and sulphur-free materials. The photographs show HSA signage alongside a sulphur-free
Dang Shen label and a separate American ginseng label. The Dang Shen label presents a
processing claim, while the American ginseng label identifies the product; together, these labels
show how specific wording may reassure shoppers. The photograph records these claims without
verifying them. In its separate guidance on health supplements, HSA also prohibits suggesting that a
product is safe simply because it is natural (HSA, 2026a). The distinction between a label’s message
and independently established product properties is therefore central to interpreting the language of
trust.
