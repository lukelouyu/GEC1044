3.3 Singapore weather and the appeal of lighter soups
Staff linked lighter soups’ appeal to Singapore’s hot, humid environment (Meteorological Service
Singapore [MSS], n.d.).
Classical texts such as the Suwen (素问) link health maintenance to adapting to seasonal cycles
(Unschuld & Tessenow, 2011, pp. 45–57). Singapore does not have the four temperate seasons, so
these principles are applied differently. In a traditional seasonal framework, Yu Zhu (玉竹) is
associated with autumn dryness (qiu zao / 秋燥): it nourishes Yin, moistens dryness and generates
fluids, and is used to protect Lung and Stomach Yin. In the shop’s Singapore setting, Yu Zhu
appears in light-nourishment soups such as the recorded Yu Zhu–Huai Shan soup. Staff described
its use in terms of gentle moistening and fluid replenishment.
