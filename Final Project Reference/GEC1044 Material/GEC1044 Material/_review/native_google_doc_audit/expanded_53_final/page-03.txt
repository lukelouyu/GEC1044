Hougang Central had about 4,620 residents in June 2025, with roughly 1,070 aged 65 or above
(23%) (DOS, 2025). Chua et al.’s (2025) 647 Hougang respondents averaged 69 years of age and
22 years’ residence. The area also has Teochew heritage around Tou Mu Kung, fishing, farming and
rubber-growing (National Heritage Board [NHB], n.d.). This established residential setting helps
explain the reported clientele, but may not represent outlets in younger neighbourhoods.
3 Everyday care and food therapy
3.1 Herbs in household meals
Staff named Dang Shen (党参), Huang Qi (黄芪), Yu Zhu (玉竹), Huai Shan (淮山), red dates (红枣)
and goji (枸杞) as common soup ingredients. Ginseng (人参), American ginseng (西洋参) and monk
fruit (罗汉果) were also selected by neighbourhood customers. Table A1 lists these nine
staff-described best-selling herbs.
Soup gives herbs a familiar household purpose. Lecture 5 describes Chinese medicine as a cultural
framework shaping everyday food and health habits (GEC1044 Lecture 5, n.d.). Nourishing life (养
生) and medicine and food sharing a source (药食同源) help explain how the display connects meals
with health maintenance. Lim et al. (2005) identify health maintenance as an important reason for
dietary herb use. Our fieldwork illustrates this through cooking: households encounter traditional
ingredients as part of preparing familiar meals, rather than only when seeking treatment.
3.2 Soup packs and light nourishment
Table A2 records five soup labels: Qing Bu Liang (清补凉), Dang Shen–Huang Qi (党参北芪补气汤),
Yu Zhu–Huai Shan (玉竹淮山润燥汤), goji–red date (枸杞红枣养生汤) and Five Finger Fig (五指毛桃
健脾汤). Staff linked them with aiding digestion (助消化), moistening (润燥) and light nourishment
(qing bu / 清补). Light nourishment offers an everyday dietary approach alongside stronger
tonification (da bu / 大补). Pre-packaged ingredients translate these ideas into household practice:
the shop selects and portions the materials, while buyers still cook the soup at home.
Several soups, especially Five Finger Fig, emphasise Spleen/digestive support. The TCM Spleen
refers to a broader functional system, rather than the anatomical organ (WHO, 2022). However, the
labels also describe moistening and general nourishment, so the range should not be reduced to
one organ system. Their differing purposes show how similar cooking formats can communicate
distinct traditional-use ideas.
