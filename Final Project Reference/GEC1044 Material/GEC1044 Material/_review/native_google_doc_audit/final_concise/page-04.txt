4 Convenience and changing ways of using products
4.1 Easier preparation for younger buyers
Staff described younger customers preferring teas, supplements and simpler preparations over raw
herbs. Table A3 lists eight product categories, including ginseng tonics, herbal teas, soup packs,
mood-support products and medicated oils.
Preselected soup packs transfer selection and portioning to sellers while households retain cooking;
capsules reduce preparation further. Staff explained ingredient uses, and a multi-buy promotion
presented packs through a familiar supermarket offer. Convenience therefore changes how
preparation work is shared, helping traditional materials fit younger buyers’ routines.
4.2 Products for daily pressures
Staff linked late nights, long work hours and prolonged phone use with demand for liver-care or
mood-support products. Nature’s Green Mind Soothe Capsules use soothing Liver-constraint
language (疏肝解郁) and are listed as Singapore-manufactured Chinese Proprietary Medicines
(CPM) (Health Sciences Authority [HSA], 2025b). These descriptions are retail associations, rather
than diagnoses or evidence of mental-illness treatment.
Kumari’s (2024) report on ADP’s 2023 survey found weekly work-related stress among 68% of
Singapore workers, compared with 61% across surveyed Asia-Pacific markets. This provides
context for product language linking TCM with daily pressures. Together with preparation
convenience, it helps explain staff-described demand for packaged products.
4.3 Preparation effort and staff-described lower-demand products
Lower-demand examples included Ban Zhi Lian, specialist herb combinations and strong tonic
formulas (Table A4). Staff associated lower appeal with unfamiliarity, preparation effort and a
preference for lighter 清补 options in Singapore’s hot, humid climate. Slower-selling items were
stocked sparingly. Prepared packs reduce these barriers by transferring selection and portioning to
the shop.
