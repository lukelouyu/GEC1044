1 Introduction
At Tong Keow’s Hougang outlet, herbs and soup packs sit alongside everyday foods. Staff described
purchases for nourishment, throat comfort, fatigue and daily routines. We argue that this retail
setting keeps Traditional Chinese Medicine (TCM) visible and usable in household life.
The report examines food therapy, convenience and local adaptation through displays, staff
accounts and public sources, without assessing clinical effectiveness or sales performance.
2 Fieldwork and the neighbourhood medicine hall
2.1 How we conducted the fieldwork
We visited Tong Keow on 13 September 2026, photographed displays and interviewed one staff
member. Transcripts, the presentation script and photographs inform this report; clear meanings are
paraphrased where transcripts contain errors.
We distinguish observations, staff accounts and our interpretations. Published sources provide
context and course concepts guide analysis. Demand descriptions reflect the interviewee’s retail
experience.
2.2 Tong Keow and its mixed retail model
Tong Keow’s profile describes a medicine hall founded in Toa Payoh in 1968 and now run by
second-generation family owners. Promotional materials feature American ginseng, monk fruit,
cooling teas, pills and plasters (Tong Keow Medical Hall, 2022; “Instagram Reel About Tong Keow
Medical Hall,” n.d.).
The company advertises registered-physician consultations, acupuncture and cupping for
indigestion, colds and sprains (“Instagram Reel About Tong Keow Medical Hall,” n.d.). We did not
verify these services or observe consultations.
Branded soup packs beside herbs, grains and foods connect the branch to the wider business and
allow shoppers to encounter herbal products during grocery purchases.
2.3 Hougang and the customers described by staff
Staff described mainly Chinese, middle-aged and older customers, including people in their forties
and fifties. Hougang had 228,140 planning-area residents in June 2024; HDB reported 57,557 flats
and 165,450 residents as of 31 March 2024. These totals cover different populations (Singapore
Department of Statistics [DOS], 2024; Housing & Development Board [HDB], 2024, pp. 11–12).
