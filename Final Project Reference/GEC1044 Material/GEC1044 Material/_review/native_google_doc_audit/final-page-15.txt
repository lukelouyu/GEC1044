5.3 Different shoppers in a shared space
The staff member described most customers as Chinese, adding that some Malay customers bought
dessert ingredients and some Indian customers bought nuts. This account suggests that everyday
food purchases may give different communities familiar reasons to enter the shop. Course materials
on senna also show that the same plant can have different meanings in Chinese and Malay
medicine (GEC1044 Lecture 5, n.d.). This offers one way to interpret the shared shopping space, but
customer interviews would be needed to understand how shoppers themselves view the products.
6 Bringing the findings together through pattern differentiation and adaptation
6.1 Retail advice and pattern differentiation
Table A1 presents ginseng as neutral and American ginseng as cool and Yin-nourishing. These
different profiles show why retail suggestions cannot replace individual assessment. Staff linked
fatigue with tonic use (Table A6), but this is a retail association rather than a clinical
recommendation. Choosing between these herbs for treatment requires a fuller assessment of signs
and symptoms through pattern differentiation and treatment (辨证论治) (WHO, 2022). The ginseng
comparison makes the principle clear: the appropriate choice depends on the person’s full pattern.
TCM use also often sits alongside other care. MOH reported that nearly 40% of TCM users in the
2022 National Population Health Survey used TCM and Western medicine for the same condition
(Ministry of Health [MOH], 2025). This gives context to the medicine hall’s everyday role: a purchase
for nourishment or household care may be one part of a wider health routine.
