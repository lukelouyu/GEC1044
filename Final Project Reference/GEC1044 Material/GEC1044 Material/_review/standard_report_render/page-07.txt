7 Limitations
The report draws on one visit and one staff interview. Accounts of demand, preferences and cross￾cultural purchases come from staff. Sales records, customer interviews and branch comparisons 
would deepen the assessment. Neighbourhood statistics describe the surrounding population, and 
industry accounts supply comparisons.
Complete soup recipes and sourcing and purchasing records were unavailable. The pictured Nature’s 
Green capsules have a confirmed Singapore manufacturing link; ingredient origins remain unknown, 
and staff named the category rather than the brand. Physician presence was unclear, and we recorded 
no consultation, prescription or patient outcome. MOH (2026) supplies the wider professional￾registration context.
The appendix distinguishes reference profiles and external images from field photographs. Customer 
interviews and further visits could deepen this account.
8 Conclusion
Tong Keow makes TCM available through food, prepared products and a mixed medicine hall. Soup 
displays connect herbs to cooking and light nourishment. Prepared packs shift selection and 
portioning work to the seller, helping households begin with cooking. Local branding and 
manufacture place some goods within Singapore’s retail setting, while the mixed food range offers 
different communities familiar reasons to enter. Together, the displays and staff accounts support our 
argument that traditional materials gain everyday uses through adaptation.
The case brings continuity through adaptation into focus at the scale of a Hougang shop. Food use 
provides a household purpose, convenience changes the effort involved, and localisation gives 
products a recognisable form. These retail adaptations sustain opportunities for people across all 
races, religions and age groups to encounter TCM. Choosing treatment remains a separate task 
requiring assessment of the person’s pattern of signs and symptoms. The shop’s role in making 
products approachable and the clinical work of individual assessment are therefore distinct parts of 
the wider practice of care.
