6 Bringing the findings together: pattern differentiation and adaptation
6.1 Retail advice and pattern differentiation
Table A1 describes ginseng as neutral and American ginseng as cool and Yin-nourishing. These 
contrasting profiles clarify the importance of individualised assessment. The staff’s pairing of fatigue 
with tonic use in Table A6 offers a starting point for discussion, but choosing between them clinically 
requires a fuller assessment of signs and symptoms through pattern differentiation and treatment (辨
证论治) (WHO, 2022). This echoes Lecture 5’s contrast between treatment approaches for cold￾damage and warm-disease patterns. The ginseng comparison makes this principle tangible: the 
appropriate choice depends on the person’s fuller clinical presentation.
These clinical distinctions also need to be understood within a broader context: modern TCM forms 
part of overlapping care practices. MOH reported that nearly 40% of TCM users in the 2022 National 
Population Health Survey combined TCM and Western medicine for the same condition (Ministry of 
Health [MOH], 2025). This national finding helps contextualise the medicine hall’s everyday role: 
buying products for nourishment or household care may be one part of a person’s wider health 
routine.
6.2 How the shop brings TCM into everyday life
The themes form a connected account of access. Food gives herbs a household purpose, packs reduce 
selection and preparation effort, and packaging communicates uses. Local branding and manufacture 
connect products to Singapore's retail and regulatory setting. We interpret these features as practical 
means of bringing traditional materials into everyday use.
The mixed shop gives these activities one location. Branding connects the branch to a wider business, 
while Hougang's residential population gives context to the clientele described by staff. These 
features help explain the fit between its shelf range and household routines. The shop's everyday role 
emerges through products, preparation and place.
Unschuld's (2018) account of adaptation and Yang's (2022) history help frame these connections. 
Unschuld argues that Chinese medicine has always adapted to its social and political environment, 
rather than remaining fixed as an unchanging "tradition". Yang shows that TCM in Singapore 
underwent a specific process of modernisation, institutionalisation and localisation, shaped by the 
state, medical professionals and religious organisations. These two perspectives help explain why the 
shop's retail practices are not simply commercial strategies, but part of a longer history of TCM
