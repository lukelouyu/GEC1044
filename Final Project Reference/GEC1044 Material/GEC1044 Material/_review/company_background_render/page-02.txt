central to its everyday role: herbal goods can be encountered during an ordinary food-shopping trip.
2.3 Hougang and the customers described by staff
Staff described customers as mainly Chinese, middle-aged and older, including people in their forties 
and fifties. Hougang planning area had 228,140 residents in June 2024. HDB separately reported 
57,557 dwelling units and 165,450 residents in its Hougang flats at 31 March 2024. These totals 
cover different housing populations (DOS, 2024; Housing & Development Board [HDB], 2024, pp. 
11–12).
Hougang Central, the subzone around the outlet, had about 4,620 residents in June 2025. Official 
rounded age-band totals put around 1,070 residents at age 65 or above, roughly 23% (DOS, 2025). 
This substantial older population provides a local setting for the clientele described by staff and for 
interpreting the shop’s household foods and nourishment products.
Chua et al.’s (2025) 647 Hougang respondents averaged 69 years of age and 22 years of residence. 
Seventeen per cent lived alone, compared with 15% of 552 Taman Jurong respondents; the difference 
was not statistically significant. The long residence is especially relevant here: it places the medicine 
hall within an established community where household shopping and care take place.
Hougang’s Teochew history adds another dimension. The National Heritage Board records the 
community around Tou Mu Kung and its fishing, farming and rubber-growing activities (National 
Heritage Board [NHB], n.d.). We interpret this history and residential setting as context for the shop’s 
mixed medicinal and food range. Staff supplied the account of its current customers.
