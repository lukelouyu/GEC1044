1 Introduction
At Tong Keow’s Hougang outlet, herbs and packaged soups sit alongside grains and everyday foods. 
Staff described purchases for nourishment, throat comfort, fatigue and concerns about daily routines. 
These shelves and accounts place Traditional Chinese Medicine (TCM) within ordinary shopping and 
cooking, the starting point of our report.
We argue that Tong Keow helps sustain TCM by fitting traditional materials into everyday household 
practices. Section 3 explains how food use gives herbs a familiar purpose. Section 4 examines how 
prepared products reduce the work of using them. Section 5 considers their fit with Singapore’s 
production, regulation and cultural setting. Section 6 brings these connections together and explains 
the role of individual assessment in treatment. Appendix A retains the detailed survey findings.
