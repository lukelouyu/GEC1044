## Lecture 10

### L10-01-title

[Source screenshot](slides/L10-01-title.jpg)

Chinese ideas of health preservation and ageing; original slide code GEH1070.

### L10-02-sheng

[Source screenshot](slides/L10-02-sheng.jpg)

养 means cultivate/preserve/nourish; 生 concerns life/health. Goals include health maintenance, prevention and longevity within a human–nature worldview.

### L10-03-yangsheng

[Source screenshot](slides/L10-03-yangsheng.jpg)

Cultivation dimensions: 养身 body, 养心 mind, 养性 character, 养神 spirit. Another framework is 精 essence, 气 breath/qi and 神 spirit.

### L10-04-mind

[Source screenshot](slides/L10-04-mind.jpg)

The seven emotions on this slide are joy, anger, sorrow, fear, love, hate and desire. Excess, prolonged immersion or sudden shock is distinguished from ordinary emotion.

### L10-05-ji-kang

[Source screenshot](slides/L10-05-ji-kang.jpg)

Ji Kang (220–265 CE) illustrates historical claims that cultivation and guiding qi can produce exceptional longevity. These are historical aspirations, not established lifespan effects.

### L10-06-daoyin

[Source screenshot](slides/L10-06-daoyin.jpg)

导引 combines guiding qi and stretching; the slide emphasizes awareness, controlled breathing and slow movement, and calls it a forerunner of qigong.

### L10-07-daoyin-chart

[Source screenshot](slides/L10-07-daoyin-chart.jpg)

Mawangdui archaeology is used to establish a long history of health-cultivation exercises; the chart is associated with 168 BCE. R15 refines the provenance to Tomb 3.

### L10-08-mawangdui

[Source screenshot](slides/L10-08-mawangdui.jpg)

A chart shows many exercise postures and links to a demonstration. The image supplies visual historical evidence, not complete movement instructions.

### L10-09-ageing

[Source screenshot](slides/L10-09-ageing.jpg)

Ageing is framed through internal/external harmony and everyday attention to seasons, diet, rest, massage, exercise and disciplined living.

### L10-10-laolao

[Source screenshot](slides/L10-10-laolao.jpg)

Cao Tingdong (1699–1785), Lao Lao Heng Yan/Lasting Advice on Growing Old; the slide says he wrote the elderly-healthcare work at 75.

### L10-11-discourses

[Source screenshot](slides/L10-11-discourses.jpg)

遵生八笺, Eight Discourses on Respecting Life (1591): Ming health writing spans medical, Daoist, Confucian, elite and household contexts.

### L10-12-hu-wenhuan

[Source screenshot](slides/L10-12-hu-wenhuan.jpg)

胡文焕, 保生心鉴/Heart Mirror for Preserving Life: an illustrated seated exercise links posture, the ears, breathing/body actions and historical therapeutic claims.

### L10-13-baosheng

[Source screenshot](slides/L10-13-baosheng.jpg)

A second illustrated practice is associated historically with rheumatism-related swelling/pain. It combines hands, posture, attention and breathing.

### L10-14-baduanjin

[Source screenshot](slides/L10-14-baduanjin.jpg)

八段锦: eight brocade/silken movements; the slide describes multiple historical origin attributions rather than a single verified creator.

### L10-15-demonstration

[Source screenshot](slides/L10-15-demonstration.jpg)

Eight labels: hold up heavens; draw bow; separate heaven/earth; gaze backward; clench fists/glare; hold feet; sway head/shake tail; bounce on toes. This preserves the slide’s order.

### L10-16-summary

[Source screenshot](slides/L10-16-summary.jpg)

The conclusion joins balance, careful eating, disciplined lifestyle and self-maintained exercise as the lecture’s health-preservation approach.
