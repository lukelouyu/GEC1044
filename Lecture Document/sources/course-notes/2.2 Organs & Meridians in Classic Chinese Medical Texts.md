

## 1. The Body as a Microcosm

In the _Inner Classic of the Yellow Emperor_ (**Huangdi Neijing 黃帝內經**), the human body is not treated as an isolated biological machine. It is a **microcosm of the state and the wider cosmos**: bodily structures correspond to political offices, Yin–Yang, the Five Agents, seasons and environmental phenomena. **Health therefore means maintaining harmony and orderly relationships within this whole system**, rather than treating each organ independently.

The body's main internal structures include the **zang 臟 (viscera/storage organs)**, **fu 腑 (bowels)**, the **meridian network (jing-mai 經脈)**, and substances such as **qi 氣, blood, essence (jing 精) and spirit (shen 神)**. Qi c**irculates rhythmically through the meridian network** and works closely with blood, which carries it throughout the body.

---

## 2. Yin–Yang Division of the Body

Chapter 4 of the _Basic Questions (Suwen 素問)_ maps the body itself onto Yin and Yang:

|Yin 陰|Yang 陽|
|---|---|
|**Interior (li 裏)**|**Exterior (biao 表)**|
|**Abdomen (fu 腹)**|**Back (bei 背)**|
|**Five zang viscera 五臟**|**Six fu bowels 六腑**|
|Heart, Liver, Spleen, Lung, Kidney|Gallbladder, Stomach, Large Intestine, Small Intestine, Bladder, Triple Burner|

The **zang organs** are associated with longer-term storage, especially of vital substances and spirit, whereas the **fu organs** function more as temporary receivers, processors and transmitters.

> [!IMPORTANT]  
> **Yin → interior → zang organs**  
> **Yang → exterior → fu organs**  
> This Yin–Yang distinction becomes the basis for matching organs with **Yin and Yang meridian channels**.

---

## 3. The Twelve Organs as an Imperial Government

The _Neijing_ explains the body using the structure of an **imperial bureaucracy**. Each organ functions like an official with a particular responsibility. The **Heart occupies the highest position**, like the emperor, while other organs coordinate and support the whole system.

|Organ|Bureaucratic Role|Main Function|
|---|---|---|
|**Heart**|Ruler / Emperor|Spirit brilliance|
|**Lung**|Chancellor and mentor|Order and moderation|
|**Liver**|General|Planning and deliberation|
|**Gallbladder**|Rectifier|Decisions and judgement|
|**Danzhong 膻中 / Pericardium**|Minister and envoy|Joy and happiness|
|**Spleen + Stomach**|Official for grain storage|Five flavours|
|**Large Intestine**|Transmitter along the Way|Changes and transformations|
|**Small Intestine**|Receiver of what has been perfected|Transformation of things|
|**Kidneys**|Official operator with force|Technical skills and expertise|
|**Triple Burner**|Operator of channels|Pathway of water|
|**Bladder**|Regional rectifier|Storage of body liquids|

This political analogy expresses an important medical idea: **organs are relational and hierarchical**. Disorder in one office can disturb the functioning of the entire bodily “state.”

### Heart-Mind 心 (_xin_)

The Heart is not simply a blood-pumping organ. **Xin 心 means both “heart” and “mind”**, and is associated with consciousness, thought, intention, emotions and desire. The brain is therefore not treated as the single central controller in this system; mental and emotional functions are distributed across the body's organs.

---

## 4. Triple Burner 三焦 (_sanjiao_)

The **Triple Burner** is one of the six fu organs but differs from ordinary anatomical organs. It is described as **“having a name but no form”** because it represents a functional system divided into three bodily regions.

|Burner|Main Contents|Main Function|
|---|---|---|
|**Upper Burner**|Heart, lungs, pericardium, throat, head|Respiration; distributes qi|
|**Middle Burner**|Spleen, stomach, gallbladder|Transforms and transports food and water|
|**Lower Burner**|Liver, kidneys, bladder, intestines, uterus|Processes fluids and separates useful from waste|

Its overall role is to **coordinate organs and the movement of qi and fluids into an integrated system**.

## 5. Five Agents and Systematic Correspondence

The **Five Agents/Phases (wuxing 五行)** organize the body together with directions, climate, flavours, organs and body tissues.

|Agent|Direction|Climatic Quality|Flavour|Zang Organ|Body Part|
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
|**Wood 木**|East|Wind|Sour|Liver|Sinews|
|**Fire 火**|South|Heat|Bitter|Heart|Blood|
|**Earth 土**|Centre|Dampness|Sweet|Spleen|Flesh|
|**Metal 金**|West|Dryness|Pungent|Lung|Skin & hair|
|**Water 水**|North|Cold|Savoury|Kidney|Bone marrow|

The table illustrates **systematic correspondence**: the Liver, for example, is not understood only anatomically but as part of a wider **Wood–East–Wind–Sour–Sinews** network. Changes in the environment and changes inside the body are therefore interpreted using the same conceptual framework.

---

## 6. From Five Agents to Six Phases

To match the **six zang organs and six fu organs**, Fire is divided into two:

- **Weak/Sovereign Fire (jun huo 君火)** → Heart
    
- **Strong/Minister Fire (xiang huo 相火)** → Pericardium / Triple Burner
    

This produces six correspondences rather than five. The political metaphor remains important: **Sovereign Fire represents the ruler**, while **Minister Fire assists the ruler**. Harmony requires each to remain in its proper position.

---

## 7. Six Degrees of Yin and Yang

Yin and Yang are not fixed opposites but exist in **different degrees of strength**. These became the **Six Phases**, which are also mapped onto meridian channels:

```text
Greater Yang (Taiyang 太陽)
        ↓
Bright Yang (Yangming 陽明)
        ↓
Lesser Yang (Shaoyang 少陽)
        ↓
Greater Yin (Taiyin 太陰)
        ↓
Lesser Yin (Shaoyin 少陰)
        ↓
Terminal Yin (Jueyin 厥陰)
```

The lecture connects these variations to the changing balance of Yin and Yang across **day and night**. The same changing cosmic rhythms are reflected within the human body through its organs and meridians.

---

## 8. Six Yin Meridian Channels — Zang Organs

The **six zang organs correspond to six Yin meridian channels**, together with their Five-Agent association.

|Zang Organ|Phase|Season|Yin Meridian|
|---|---|---|---|
|**Liver**|Wood|Spring|Foot Jueyin Liver — **足厥陰肝經**|
|**Heart**|Weak Fire|Summer|Hand Shaoyin Heart — **手少陰心經**|
|**Pericardium**|Strong Fire|Summer|Hand Jueyin Pericardium — **手厥陰心包經**|
|**Spleen**|Earth|Long Summer|Foot Taiyin Spleen — **足太陰脾經**|
|**Lung**|Metal|Autumn|Hand Taiyin Lung — **手太陰肺經**|
|**Kidney**|Water|Winter|Foot Shaoyin Kidney — **足少陰腎經**|

### Example: Lung Meridian

The **Hand Taiyin Lung Meridian (手太陰肺經)** runs along the arm and is connected to the Lung zang organ.

Its name already identifies its position:

**Hand 手 + Taiyin 太陰 + Lung 肺 + Meridian 經**

Thus, meridian names encode **location + Yin/Yang phase + associated organ**.

---

## 9. Six Yang Meridian Channels — Fu Organs

The six fu organs correspond in the same way to **Yang meridian channels**.

|Fu Organ|Phase|Yang Meridian|
|---|---|---|
|**Large Intestine**|Metal|Hand Yangming Large Intestine — **手陽明大腸經**|
|**Triple Burner**|Strong Fire|Hand Shaoyang Triple Burner — **手少陽三焦經**|
|**Small Intestine**|Weak Fire|Hand Taiyang Small Intestine — **手太陽小腸經**|
|**Stomach**|Earth|Foot Yangming Stomach — **足陽明胃經**|
|**Gallbladder**|Wood|Foot Shaoyang Gallbladder — **足少陽膽經**|
|**Bladder**|Water|Foot Taiyang Bladder — **足太陽膀胱經**|

> [!IMPORTANT]  
> **Zang organs → Yin meridians**  
> **Fu organs → Yang meridians**

---

## 10. Meridian Channels and Acupuncture

Meridian channels can be imagined as **pathways or rivers through which qi circulates**. Along each channel are named **acupuncture points (xue 穴)**. Because an organ is connected to its meridian, treatment does **not necessarily occur directly over the anatomical organ**.

For example, a disorder associated with the **Large Intestine or Stomach system** may be treated by inserting needles into appropriate points **along their corresponding meridian channels**, including points located on the limbs.

---

## Core Idea

> [!ABSTRACT]  
> The _Huangdi Neijing_ understands the body through **systematic correspondence**:
> 
> **Cosmos ↔ State ↔ Human Body**  
> **Yin–Yang ↔ Zang/Fu ↔ Meridian Channels**  
> **Five/Six Phases ↔ Seasons ↔ Organs ↔ Tissues ↔ Environment**
> 
> Chinese medicine therefore treats the body as an **interconnected, dynamic system**. Health depends on maintaining correct relationships, movement and harmony among all of its parts rather than viewing organs as isolated anatomical structures.
