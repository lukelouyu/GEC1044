### 1. Two teaching analogies: gardener and mechanic

**1:42–5:33.** The lecture contrasts a TCM doctor as a gardener with a Western-medicine doctor as a mechanic. The gardener image emphasizes cultivating a living system and its surrounding conditions; the mechanic image emphasizes investigating and repairing a malfunction. This interpretation explains the imagery rather than reproducing an unavailable spoken explanation. The reference on the comparison slide is *Between Heaven and Earth* by Harriet Beinfield and Efrem Korngold.

The next slide connects the human life cycle to nature through yin–yang and seasonal processes:

| Seasonal image | Process shown | Place in the conceptual cycle |
|---|---|---|
| Spring | Sprouting, 生 | Emergence and growth |
| Summer | Maturing, 长 | Expansion and development |
| Intermediate phase | Ripening, 化 | Transformation/maturation |
| Autumn | Harvesting, 收 | Gathering and consolidation |
| Winter | Composting/storage, 藏 | Conservation and preparation for renewal |

The Western-medicine slide pairs the mechanic with a drawing labelled “Body as Machine.” These are simplified conceptual contrasts. They do not establish that either system exclusively uses one approach or that one system is superior.

### 2. What integration looks like in Chinese hospitals

**7:57–19:30.** Two teaching-hospital examples are shown: **Dongfang Hospital, Beijing University of Chinese Medicine's Second Affiliated Teaching Hospital**, and **Tianjin University of TCM's First Affiliated Teaching Hospital**.

The hospital photographs place emergency services, radiographic facilities and a **治未病 preventive-treatment centre** within the same institutional setting. The point visible in the slides is coexistence of biomedical infrastructure and TCM services, not the exclusion of modern investigations from a TCM hospital.

Training facilities include an **electronic pulse machine** and displays of **tongue photographs**. These show the use of teaching aids and technology in learning traditional assessments; a photograph of a device does not establish its diagnostic accuracy.

Outpatient departments listed include cardiology, neurology, nephrology, endocrinology, rheumatology and immunology. A dedicated acupuncture department is highlighted. The lecture therefore presents institutional TCM as organized across multiple clinical specialties.

**The outpatient case-record slide is the clearest practical integration example.** It combines:

- Main symptoms and history of the present illness.
- Past medical, personal and family histories.
- Tongue colour/coating and pulse assessment.
- Blood pressure and pulse rate.
- Kidney-profile and blood-glucose results.
- CT brain imaging.

The slide demonstrates the kinds of information assembled; the handwritten patient record is not sufficiently clear to reconstruct an individual diagnosis or treatment outcome.

The prescription slide describes a **combination of herbs in specified ratios and doses**, containing multiple compounds. A formula is therefore not simply an interchangeable list of plant names. Exact ingredients and doses in the handwritten example are not inferred.

For inpatients, the slide describes **daily rounds by the attending physician and interns**, vital-sign checks, lung auscultation and questions about daily symptoms. It also describes a **weekly major round by a chief/senior physician** and a specialized acupuncture department.

### 4. Research, classification and prevention

**1:14:06.** The scientific-contribution example is **Tu Youyou's 2015 Nobel Prize for Physiology or Medicine** and the discovery of artemisinin, an antimalarial compound associated with qinghao. The following two sidebar entries are frames from an embedded news clip, not additional text slides. The defensible learning point is the example of traditional knowledge contributing to modern drug research; it does not validate every herbal formulation by analogy.

**1:18:12.** A WHO/ICD-11 slide reports adoption in 2019 and implementation in 2022, and lists traditional-medicine disorder and pattern categories. Its stated rationale is recording and classifying information about traditional-medicine presentations. **A classification system enables documentation and epidemiology; it is not an efficacy endorsement for every intervention.**

**1:19:15.** The next slide shows the WHO expert-meeting report evaluating TCM in COVID-19 treatment, dated 31 March 2022. It describes a meeting held from 28 February to 2 March 2022. The screenshot establishes the report's presence and purpose; its findings cannot be inferred from the cover/overview alone.

**1:20:45.** A slide cites an October 2022 Healthier SG parliamentary speech emphasizing common ground in prevention: managing risk factors and disease early to reduce later complications.

**1:21:51.** The final slide shows a forum on evidence-based TCM in Western practice. The photographed presentation concerns **standardizing outcomes in TCM intervention studies for chronic heart failure**, including challenges and a core outcome set. This connects integration with consistent research measurement.

### Additional research — integration, translation and current assessment

**Traditional knowledge can generate a testable lead.** Tu Youyou's own account describes moving from herbal extracts to a reproducible active fraction, isolation of artemisinin, chemical characterization and further drug-development work. It also emphasizes identifying the correct plant species and preparation. This adds a concrete research pathway to the lecture's Nobel example: **historical observation → candidate selection → controlled extraction → identification → testing → standardized medicine**. Evidence about the isolated product does not automatically transfer to any tea, dose or multi-herb mixture. [R1: Tu Youyou’s scientific account](https://www.nobelprize.org/prizes/medicine/2015/tu/biographical/)

**Integration also needs compatible information.** WHO explains that ICD-11 traditional-medicine categories support standardized reporting and optional dual coding alongside conventional diagnoses. WHO explicitly separates classification from endorsement of a practice or intervention. Thus an institutional milestone and a clinical-effectiveness finding answer different questions. [R2: WHO explanation of traditional-medicine coding](https://www.who.int/standards/classifications/frequently-asked-questions/traditional-medicine)

**Current update, checked 4 November 2026.** TCMPB's 5 October 2026 announcement confirms that modified STRE began in August 2025 and applied in 2026, with the first Clinical Competency Assessment scheduled for 2027. This verifies the slide's direction of change. The announcement also distinguishes central administration in 2027 from school administration with Board oversight from 2028. These details are external updates, not additional words recovered from the recording. [R3: TCMPB October 2026 announcement](https://www.tcmpb.gov.sg/publications-and-newsroom/announcements/implementation-of-the-clinical-competency-assessment--cca--for-the-tcmpb-qualifying-examinations-for-registration/)

**Research question to retain:** Does a proposed integrated service improve an outcome patients value, compared with a clearly defined alternative, while monitoring harms? Co-location, professional recognition and a shared database can enable a service; their existence alone does not answer that clinical question. This is an interpretation connecting the lecture to research design.
